Design exploration · Notion theme variants

What do the colours and dots mean?

Eight Notion-theme variants split across two questions. The first half pins down what pastel card backgrounds encode; the second pins down what the scattered hero dots encode. Pick one from each — the answers compose into a single visual language.

Colour-tint semantics — what does each pastel MEAN?

Pastel card backgrounds are Notion's most distinctive move. They have to mean something or they read as decoration. Four candidate rules.

Variant 01 · colours-A

Cut-only (lavender = Editor's Cut)

Rule: exactly one tint is semantic — lavender = Editor's Cut commentary. Every other card stays white. Maximally restrained; the cut is the only visual island.

Variant 02 · colours-B

Story-type (one tint per category)

Rule: tint encodes kind of story — lavender = Editor's Cut, mint = product launch / model release, peach = opinion, sky = tooling / dev post, yellow = research paper, cream = community thread.

Judge A · MEANING_AT_A_GLANCE 7/10 · rich but legend-dependent Judge B · MEANING_AT_A_GLANCE 8/10 · scannable once learned; teaches the section structure

Variant 03 · colours-C

Heat-tier (importance encoded)

Rule: tint encodes importance heat — rose = breaking, peach = major release, mint = worth-reading, white = neutral (the default ~80%). Inverted pyramid; tinted cards are sparse by design.

Judge A · MEANING_AT_A_GLANCE 9/10 · reader instantly knows what to read first Judge B · MEANING_AT_A_GLANCE 8/10 · assumes the editor can rank truthfully every day

Variant 04 · colours-D

Neutral (badge-only, no tinted cards)

Rule: cards stay white; source identity lives in the badge colour, and Editor's Cut is signalled by a purple left-border + badge. Tint never carries meaning — it's deliberately absent.

Hero-dot semantics — what do the dots MEAN?

The scattered pastel dots in the Notion hero are the second contested signal. Either they encode something useful, or they should go. Four candidates.

Variant 05 · dots-A

No dots (typography-only hero)

Rule: no dots at all. The hero leans entirely on display type and the live-edition kicker. Removes the “what do they mean?” question by removing the dots.

Judge A · MEANING_AT_A_GLANCE 10/10 · nothing to misread Judge B · MEANING_AT_A_GLANCE 7/10 · loses the Notion signature visual
Open full page →
Variant 06 · dots-B

Date strip (week's top-story types)

Rule: seven dots = the last seven days. Each dot's colour = the top-story type for that day (rose = lab post, peach = model drop, mint = products, sky = industry, cream = research, yellow = trending, lavender = today's cut). Today's dot is enlarged.

Judge A · MEANING_AT_A_GLANCE 8/10 · timeline metaphor lands fast Judge B · MEANING_AT_A_GLANCE 7/10 · dual encoding (day + type) needs the day-label crutch
Open full page →
Variant 07 · dots-C

Section mix (today's section anchors)

Rule: six dots = the six sections in today's edition (lavender = model drops, mint = products, sky = industry, cream = research, neutral = lab posts, yellow = GitHub trending). Each dot is an anchor link to its section.

Judge A · MEANING_AT_A_GLANCE 8/10 · doubles as a nav skeleton Judge B · MEANING_AT_A_GLANCE 9/10 · clearest payoff — click goes somewhere
Open full page →
Variant 08 · dots-D

Subscribe-anchor (dots above CTA)

Rule: same section-mix mapping as dots-C, but the dot strip is repositioned to sit directly above the subscribe CTA — visually anchoring “here's what you'd get” to the convert action.
